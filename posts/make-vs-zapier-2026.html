Make vs Zapier 2026: Which Automation Tool Actually Wins?

Visual workflow power vs polished simplicity — we break down pricing, features, AI integrations, and who each platform is really for.

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read · By AI Tools EN

Two tools dominate the no-code automation space in 2026: Make (formerly Integromat) and Zapier. They both connect your apps and automate repetitive tasks — but they approach it very differently, serve different users, and have wildly different price points.

Zapier built its reputation on simplicity. It's the tool that made automation accessible to non-technical users. Make built its reputation on raw power — complex branching logic, visual workflows, and dramatically cheaper pricing for serious use.

After testing both platforms extensively, here's the honest breakdown.

Interface & Ease of Use

This is where Zapier has its clearest advantage. Setting up a Zap takes minutes — you pick a trigger app, pick an action app, map your fields, and you're done. The UI is clean, the prompts are clear, and Zapier does a lot of hand-holding that new users genuinely appreciate.

Make's interface is a visual canvas — your workflow is a diagram of connected modules that you see all at once. It looks impressive and gives you a clear mental model of what's happening, but it requires more upfront learning. The first time you try to set up error handling or use Make's iterator module, you'll probably open the documentation.

The trade-off

Zapier's simplicity is a feature, not a limitation — for the right user. If you run a small business, manage your own marketing stack, and want automation that "just works," Zapier's linear flow is genuinely easier to maintain and debug.

For anyone building more than 5-10 automations or handling complex data pipelines, Make's canvas pays for itself. Being able to see your entire workflow at a glance — including all branches, routers, and error handlers — makes debugging and modification dramatically faster.

Workflow Power & Logic

This is Make's home turf, and it's not close.

Make's advanced features

  • Routers: Send data down different paths based on conditions — like a real if/else branch
  • Iterators & aggregators: Process arrays of data (e.g., loop through all rows in a spreadsheet, process each one, collect results)
  • Error handlers: Define what happens when a module fails — retry, continue, rollback
  • Data stores: A lightweight built-in database for storing and reading values across scenarios
  • Custom functions: Write JavaScript functions to transform data inside your scenarios
  • Scenario linking: Trigger one scenario from another for modular automation architecture

Zapier's logic

Zapier has Paths (branching), Filters, and Formatter (data transformation) — but they're more limited. Paths work well for simple A/B routing, but building complex multi-branch logic quickly becomes unwieldy. Zapier's data transformation tools are basic compared to Make's formula editor.

For a simple "when new email arrives → add row to sheet → send Slack message" workflow, Zapier is perfectly fine. For "when customer submits form → validate data → route to different teams based on 5 conditions → update CRM → send personalized email sequence → log results," you want Make.

Integrations & App Coverage

Zapier wins on raw numbers: 7,000+ integrations vs Make's 1,500+. This matters if you use niche or less-popular tools — there's a real chance Zapier has a native integration where Make requires a workaround via HTTP/webhooks.

In practice, Make's 1,500+ apps cover the vast majority of what businesses actually use. Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Notion, Airtable, HubSpot, Salesforce, Stripe, Shopify, WordPress — it's all there. The HTTP module in Make also makes connecting to any REST API trivial, even without a native integration.

AI Capabilities in 2026

Both platforms have leaned hard into AI integrations, and both handle the major models well. The difference is in how they let you use AI in workflows.

Make + AI

Make's canvas is genuinely well-suited to complex AI pipelines. You can build workflows where a trigger fires, data is transformed, passed to OpenAI or Claude with a structured prompt, the response is parsed and routed based on content, different follow-up actions happen in parallel — all visualized in one place. Make's JSON parsing and data transformation tools make working with AI API responses much less painful than in Zapier.

Zapier + AI

Zapier has its own AI features branded as "Zapier AI" — including the ability to describe a Zap in plain English and have it auto-built. For common use cases (summarize email → draft reply → send for approval), this is genuinely useful. Zapier's AI assistant in the builder is more polished than Make's.

Make has a clear edge for complex AI workflows. Zapier wins for simple AI-powered Zaps you can set up in minutes via natural language.

Final Verdict

🏆 Make wins for power users. Zapier wins for beginners.

If you're serious about automation — multiple workflows, complex logic, AI pipelines, or agency-level scale — Make is the better choice. It's cheaper, more powerful, and gives you full visibility into what your automations are actually doing. The learning curve is real but it's a one-time investment.

If you're just starting with automation, have non-technical team members who need to maintain workflows, or need a very specific niche integration, Zapier's ease of use is genuinely valuable. It's not the wrong choice — it's just expensive once you grow.

Our recommendation: Start with Make if you have any technical comfort. The savings alone justify the learning curve within the first month. If Make genuinely feels too complex after a week, Zapier will be there.

Alternatives Worth Knowing

  • n8n — Open-source, self-hostable, free on your own server. Comparable power to Make, zero recurring cost if you self-host. Ideal for developers.
  • Pabbly Connect — One-time payment option, unlimited workflows. Worth checking if you hate SaaS subscriptions.
  • Microsoft Power Automate — Best choice if you're deep in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem. Included with many M365 plans.
  • Activepieces — Open-source Zapier alternative, growing fast in 2026.
  • Relay.app — AI-native automation built for human-in-the-loop workflows. Good for teams that want AI suggestions and approval steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Make better than Zapier in 2026?

Make is more powerful and affordable — it handles complex, branching workflows with far more flexibility per dollar. Zapier is easier to set up and better for simple, linear automations. If you're comfortable with a visual canvas and want serious workflow power, Make wins.

How does Make pricing compare to Zapier?

Make is significantly cheaper. Make's Core plan is $9/month for 10,000 operations. Zapier's Starter is $19.99/month for 750 tasks. For power users, Make delivers 5-10x the value per dollar at equivalent usage levels.

Can Make replace Zapier completely?

For most use cases, yes. Make covers all the major integrations and adds HTTP/webhook support on all plans. The main reasons to stay on Zapier are niche app coverage (7,000+ integrations) and team members who need the simpler interface.

Does Make work with AI tools in 2026?

Yes — Make has native integrations for OpenAI, Anthropic Claude, and Google Gemini. Its visual canvas is particularly well-suited to complex AI pipelines with routing, conditional logic, and structured data handling.

Is Zapier still worth it in 2026?

Zapier is worth it for non-technical users who prioritize simplicity, and for anyone who needs a very specific niche integration. For serious automation workloads, the cost disadvantage is hard to justify compared to Make.

What's the difference between Make operations and Zapier tasks?

Make counts each module execution as one operation. Zapier counts each action step as one task (triggers are free). A three-step workflow uses ~3 operations in Make and 2 tasks in Zapier. Make's generous operation counts still make it cheaper overall.

Which automation tool is best for agencies in 2026?

Make is the clear winner for agencies. Its Teams plan, dramatically lower cost at scale, and visual canvas make it ideal for managing complex automations across multiple clients.

What are the best alternatives to Make and Zapier?

n8n (open-source, free self-hosted), Pabbly Connect (one-time payment), Microsoft Power Automate (Microsoft 365 shops), Activepieces (open-source), and Relay.app (AI-native). n8n is particularly compelling for technical teams who want unlimited automations at zero recurring cost.
